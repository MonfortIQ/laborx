How Often Should You Check Your Blood Sugar?

Expert guidelines for diabetes screening and monitoring frequency.

For individuals managing diabetes, or those at risk of developing it, monitoring blood sugar levels is a critical daily task. But exactly how often should you be checking? The answer varies significantly depending on your specific health profile, treatment plan, and lifestyle.

General Guidelines

If you take insulin to manage Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes, your doctor may recommend testing multiple times a day—often before meals, before exercise, before bed, and occasionally during the night.

  • Fasting Blood Sugar: Tested in the morning before eating.
  • Postprandial: Tested 1-2 hours after a meal to see how food affects glucose levels.
  • HbA1c: A lab test done every 3-6 months to measure your average blood sugar over a 90-day period.

The Role of Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGMs)

Modern technology has revolutionized diabetes care. Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGMs) use a tiny sensor under the skin to track glucose levels in real-time, sending data directly to a smartphone. This reduces the need for frequent finger pricks and provides a much more comprehensive view of how diet and exercise affect your sugar levels throughout the day.
